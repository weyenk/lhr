Moving to a new city is always an exercise in mapping out your personal "flavor geography." Since arriving in Atlanta, I've been on a mission to find that one Chinese spot that hits every high note—the perfect balance of salt, sweet, and that unmistakable "breath of the wok." While this city has plenty to offer, I haven't quite found the "excellent" Chinese food that matches my cravings just yet.

Instead of settling for mediocre takeout, I decided to invest in the source. I recently picked up an Iwatani FW35 portable gas grill and a heavy-duty carbon steel wok. There is something incredibly grounding about the ritual of seasoning a new wok and hearing the roar of a high-output burner. It turns the kitchen from a place of utility into a place of craft.

For my first real test of the new setup, I went back to a classic: Sesame Chicken. I took inspiration from the legends over at *The Woks of Life*, but with a crucial pivot. While many recipes suggest baking for health, I wanted that authentic, craggy texture that only a deep fry can provide. The result was a revelation—crispy, sticky, and far better than anything I could have ordered from a menu.

## Crispy Sesame Chicken

*Yields 3-4 servings · Prep 20 minutes · Cook 15 minutes*

### Pro-Tip: The Beauty of Carbon Steel

The secret to this dish isn't just the fry—it's the heat retention of the carbon steel wok. Because the Iwatani burner puts out such a concentrated flame, the wok stays incredibly hot even when you add the sauce. This allows the sugars in the honey and brown sugar to caramelize almost instantly, creating a lacquer-like finish that stays crispy rather than getting soggy. If you're new to carbon steel, don't be afraid of the high heat; that's exactly where the magic happens.
