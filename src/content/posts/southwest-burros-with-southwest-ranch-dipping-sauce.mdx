steps:
  - "Make the dipping sauce first so it has time to chill: combine the mayonnaise, sour cream, buttermilk, cilantro, lime juice, chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, onion powder, cayenne (if using), salt, and pepper in a blender and blend until smooth."
  - "Refrigerate the sauce for at least a couple of hours to let the flavors come together while you make the filling."
  - "Warm the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the diced red and green bell peppers, red onion, and jalapeño (if using), and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly browned at the edges, about 5-7 minutes."
  - "Stir in the black beans and corn and cook for another 2-3 minutes, just until heated through."
  - "Add the shredded chicken, chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, cayenne (if using), salt, and pepper. Stir until everything is evenly coated and cook for 2-3 more minutes."
  - "Lower the heat and stir in the Monterey Jack and pepper jack cheeses until fully melted into the filling."
  - "Take the skillet off the heat, fold in the fresh cilantro, and let the filling cool slightly before you start rolling."
  - "Lay a tortilla flat and spoon about a quarter of the filling into the center. Fold in both ends, then roll it up snugly to seal it into a burro."
  - "Repeat with the remaining tortillas, then set the rolled burros seam-side down in a dry skillet over low heat. Cook, turning occasionally, until the tortillas are golden and crisp on all sides, about 8-10 minutes."
  - "Slice in half if you like, and serve warm with the chilled Southwest ranch sauce on the side for dipping."

These Southwest burros take everything good about a loaded Southwest egg roll and make it easier on a weeknight — a flour tortilla and a hot skillet stand in for the deep fryer, so you still get a crisp, golden exterior around a cheesy, spiced filling, with a lot less cleanup.

The filling itself is a meal on its own — peppers, onion, black beans, corn, and shredded chicken, seasoned and bound together with two kinds of melted cheese. Rolled up tight and crisped in a dry pan, it turns into something closer to a quesadilla's crunchier cousin. The Southwest ranch on the side is what pulls it all together: cool, tangy, and just spicy enough to keep you reaching for another dip.

**Pro Tip:** Press down gently on each burro with a spatula while it crisps in the skillet — it helps the tortilla make full contact with the pan for an even, golden crust on every side.

## Southwest Burros with Southwest Ranch Dipping Sauce

**Tips & Variations:**

- Swap in rotisserie chicken to save a step, or leave the chicken out entirely for a vegetarian version.
- Use whole wheat or low-carb tortillas for a lighter take.
- Extra filling keeps in the fridge for a few days — it's just as good on its own over rice or piled into a bowl.

However you serve them, don't skip the extra dunk in that ranch — it's the best part.
